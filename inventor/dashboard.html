Overview

The concept

An inventor that never stops inventing

Inventor-Argon starts from nothing but atoms and elements. A neural brain proposes recipes, a panel of AI judges checks the physics, and everything that survives becomes a building block for the next, more ambitious idea.

The pipeline
Step by step
1

Start from primitives

The system begins with only subatomic particles and elements. Everything else, from compounds to alloys to machines, has to be invented, not hard-coded.

T0T1
2

The brain proposes

Parallel inventor workers share one GPU neural network. It learns which ingredients combine well and which recipes tend to be valid, then drafts new blueprints.

3

Blueprints are concrete

A blueprint lists ingredients and ratios, each process step with its temperature, pressure and atmosphere, and the equipment it needs. Nothing vague survives.

4

Three judges vote

Three independent AI judges check the thermodynamics, stoichiometry and physics. A recipe needs a majority; split votes block hallucinations before they reach the library.

5

Learn and escalate

Accepted items join the library and unlock higher tiers. Novel ones go to a chief-scientist deep audit for you to approve. Rejections become training signal.

The six tiers of invention

Each tier is built from the one below. Reaching a new tier usually requires an equipment gate to be unlocked first.

T0 Subatomic particles
T1 Elements
T2 Molecules & compounds
T3 Alloys, ceramics & glasses
T4 Engineered components
T5 Mechanisms & machines
Equipment gates: tools to make tools
You can’t smelt iron without a furnace. Six technology gates model that: a gate unlocks once the library contains the item that enables it, and only then can recipes that depend on that equipment be proposed. Track them on the Brain & Gates page.
Known versus novel
Known real-world items match something that exists in textbooks or industry. Novel frontier items pass the physics checks but don’t match the known catalog, so they’re escalated for a deeper audit. You decide the outcome: approve a breakthrough, mark it a plausible frontier construct, reclassify it as known, or reject it.
Why three judges?
A single language model can be confidently wrong. Asking three different models and requiring a majority makes hallucinated chemistry much less likely to slip through. The Judge Decisions page shows every vote; the dots on each row show how each judge voted.
Reading the dashboard numbers
Judge hit rate is the share of proposals the panel accepts, and should climb as the brain learns. Brain steps counts training updates; validity accuracy and property MAE show how well the brain predicts what the judges will decide.
